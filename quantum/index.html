Why any of this actually matters

Why it's genuinely cool: Grover's and Shor's algorithms aren't hype — they're two of a small handful of quantum algorithms with a real, provable mathematical speed advantage over any classical computer, at any size. Nobody has built a machine fast enough to use them at real-world scale. That's the entire gap between what you just saw and reality.
Jobs, in time: nobody is hiring "quantum programmers" at scale yet — the machines aren't there. But the run-up already is: quantum algorithm research roles exist today, security teams are re-architecting encryption years early, and reading a classical-vs-quantum trade-off is becoming a real skill in finance and tech. Most estimates put the machines that matter 10–20 years out — which means understanding this now is a head start, not a party trick.
The one to actually worry about: "store now, decrypt later." Data encrypted today can be captured and kept, unreadable, until a quantum computer able to run Shor's algorithm exists to unlock it. That's why banks and governments are already switching to quantum-safe encryption, years before the machines that would justify the panic.

None of this ran on a real quantum computer tonight — the maths is real, a machine fast enough to beat a laptop at it isn't. Not yet.

True randomness.

A classical "random" number is a formula in disguise — give it the same seed twice and you get the same numbers twice. A quantum one genuinely isn't — there's no seed and nothing to replay.
